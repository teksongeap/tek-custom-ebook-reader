IV

The small hours

The lamp had burned low. Beyond the window, the trees moved against a sky that was beginning, almost imperceptibly, to lighten.

She set the letter beside the empty cup and listened. Somewhere below the hill, the river was finding its way through the dark.

For a moment, the house seemed to be listening too.

· · ·
V

Morning at the river

By morning the rain had stopped. She took the narrow path down from the house, past the wet branches and the garden gate.

The river was wider than she remembered. Light gathered in its shallows, and a small boat waited where the path came to an end.

There was still time, she thought, to decide where to go.
